Estimating hitter contact ability

A simple model estimates a hitter’s chance of making contact when they swing, adjusting for pitch context and opponent quality. Pooling information across contexts and shrinking player effects toward the population mean stabilize estimates for limited samples. Adding batter and pitcher effects improved held-out probability predictions, while uncertainty intervals describe how precisely ability is estimated.

94,495 pitches · 49,472 swings · 200 batters · 200 pitchers

2. The supplied distribution is the modeling target

The data show extreme platoon differences: in-zone contact is about 45–50% in same-side matchups and 88–90% in opposite-side matchups. The pattern also appears out of zone. I treated these outcomes as ground truth rather than correcting them toward customary baseball expectations.

Those differences raise substantial concerns about representativeness. The estimates describe this supplied player and pitch distribution; their calibration should not be assumed to transfer to actual MLB play. Each batter has one observed batting side, so switch-hitting is absent from this dataset. Conditional pitch independence is also an imperfect assumption: without game, plate-appearance, or date information, the dependence structure is difficult to address.

3. A regularized logistic model with shared player effects

On swung-at pitches, contact outcomes follow a Bernoulli likelihood with probability:

P(contact | swing) = logistic(αcontext + ubatter + vpitcher)

The population component contains all 16 combinations of batting side, throwing side, IZ/OOZ, and FB/non-FB. Jointly estimated batter and pitcher intercepts move predictions above or below that context baseline. A pitcher who allows more contact than expected raises the predicted contact rate for the same hitter and context.

Gaussian penalties regress sparse player effects toward the mean. Player intercepts are shared across contexts, so non-fastball observations inform fastball estimates and observations against RHP inform LHP estimates. Context logits have N(0, 2.5²) priors; player effects have N(0, 1/precision) priors. A mode fit and Laplace approximation provide a modest Bayesian interpretation without MCMC.

Validation evaluates batter and pitcher precisions from 0.01 to 100. Among settings within 0.001 log loss of the best score, the rule selects the smallest summed prior variance, favoring stronger shrinkage when performance is nearly equivalent. Both selected precisions are 1. Player effects are centered within handedness for neutral-opponent interpretation.

4. Held-out performance and calibration

A 60/20/20 split of swings is stratified by the 16 contexts and retains every batter and pitcher in each partition: 29,682 training, 9,895 validation, and 9,895 test swings. After selection, models are refit on training plus validation. Test swings remain outside fitting, tuning, and reference weights.

Log loss, Brier score, and AUC improve as context and player information are added. The final model’s mean test prediction is 60.6%, versus 61.0% observed contact. Assessment also covers all 16 contexts, marginal splits, player sample-size groups, and batter-balanced scores; the largest absolute context-level mean error is 5.3 percentage points.

A pair-grouped development check keeps each pitcher–batter combination entirely within one partition. Its final-model log loss is 0.453, compared with 0.446 in the primary test. This supports robustness to the split choice, but is a sensitivity check: shrinkage selection already used development outcomes. Without dates or game identifiers, both designs assess held-out swings involving known players, rather than validated future-season forecasts or game-independent performance.

5. What the uncertainty measures cover

A local Gaussian approximation around the fitted posterior mode supplies joint coefficient draws, preserving covariance among context, batter, and pitcher coefficients. Each draw is transformed into probabilities and averaged over the fixed reference mixture. The reported bounds are approximate 95% credible intervals conditional on the chosen model and shrinkage settings.

Source of uncertaintyTreatment in this analysis
Parameter uncertaintyIncluded through joint coefficient draws, reflecting finite information under the specified model.
Bernoulli outcome randomnessIncluded in replicated contact counts and predictive checks, rather than in intervals for the underlying probability alone.
Model and selection uncertaintyArchitecture, selected penalties, and reference weights are held fixed. Misspecification and uncertainty in choosing them are not propagated.
Measurement and sampling uncertaintyLabel/feature error, nonrepresentative sampling, and changing future pitch selection are not separately modeled. Finite-sample parameter uncertainty is included only under the model assumptions.

A recovery simulation using the development matchup structure and selected Gaussian scales covered 95.5% of evaluated player targets across 20 replications. This supports the approximation under the assumed hierarchy, but does not establish coverage under distribution shift, pitch dependence, or model-selection uncertainty.

6. Additional information and future improvements

  • Pitch traits and count: velocity, movement/break, release, exact location, and count would let adjustment reflect pitch quality more directly, rather than relying on pitcher identity as a proxy.
  • Dates and game identifiers: use chronological validation for future projections and keep each game’s observations together when splitting.
  • Larger, more representative samples: assess the unusual platoon patterns and broaden coverage before transferring estimates to MLB. More data would also support partially pooled player-specific platoon, fastball, and zone effects.
  • Richer models when justified: with more data and features, evaluate a neural network with a KL-divergence objective, similar to my previous work with the Pirates, against this simpler baseline. For binary contact targets, that objective is closely related to the log-loss objective already used here.
  • A swing model: integrate P(swing) to distinguish pitch selection from contact ability and support changing future swing-context mixtures.

Another question to explore: How much of the observed contact-rate variation reflects the pitches hitters choose to swing at, versus their contact ability on comparable pitches? A joint swing/contact analysis with richer pitch features would help investigate that distinction.
